01 / The problem · a 10-minute coop story

The chicken
& egg problem.

We find eggs in a shared nest.
How do we know which hen laid each one?

Camera → evidence → events

The hens are lovely. Their record-keeping is terrible.

02 / What we want to know

Three questions.
One overhead camera.

01 / WHO

Who is in the nest?

Recognize configured hens by appearance. Keep “unknown” when the image is ambiguous.

02 / WHAT

How many visible eggs?

Count only when the nest is hen-free. A hen is a very effective privacy curtain.

03 / WHEN

What changed?

Turn repeated observations into visits, inferred laying events, and collections—with supporting photos.

The goal: a timestamped dataset and an inspectable account of which hen likely produced each egg.

03 / The pipeline

From coop cam to receipts.

01Capture

Scheduled snapshots from an overhead LAN RTSP camera.

02Recognize

Estimate hen presence and identity from reviewed examples.

03Count

Estimate visible eggs in chicken-free nests.

04Confirm

Track visits and changes across consecutive observations.

05Record

Keep journal evidence; publish Home Assistant sensors over MQTT.

A snapshot is an observation.
An event needs a little more evidence.

We do not let one blurry feather file the incident report.

04 / Identity without assigned seating

Recognize the plumage.
Not the postcode.

Use appearance

Compare brightness and texture patterns with human-reviewed reference images. Include variation in pose and lighting, plus hen-free background examples.

Reject shortcuts

Nest position, coordinates, and body orientation are not identity features. Missing reference coverage is not proof of who is sitting there.

“Unknown” is a useful answer.
A confident guess is still a guess in a nice hat.

Poses can hide distinctive markings. We retain uncertainty rather than forcing every frame into a named category.

05 / Egg attribution, illustrated

Before. Visitor. After.
A plausible producer.

BEFORE1 egg

A hen-free count establishes the baseline.

DURINGHen A

A confirmed visit. Freeze the baseline; ignore hidden eggs.

AFTER2 eggs

Confirm departure and establish the post-visit count.

INFERENCE+1 egg

Attribute the increase to the eligible confirmed visitor.

Illustrative example; “Hen A” is synthetic. Attribution depends on valid baseline, visit, departure, and count evidence. It is not direct footage of laying.

Less facial recognition. More breakfast accounting.

06 / A little patience, fewer false alarms

The nest has a memory.

3 CONSECUTIVE SHOTS

Confirm arrival

Arrivals and identity changes need repeated agreement. Unknown observations do not erase a confirmed visitor.

5 CHICKEN-FREE SHOTS

Confirm departure

A quick movement is not a checkout. Departure confirmation does not require egg-count agreement.

3 ZERO-EGG SHOTS

Confirm collection

Repeated hen-free zero counts confirm full collection. Brief dips do not reset the egg baseline.

Delayed count confirmation can support a later laying event. Ambiguous hen swaps or missing baselines keep attribution unknown; partial collection is not supported.

The chickens have no patience for paperwork. Fortunately, the software does.

08 / Learning without believing our own hype

Predict. Review.
Then teach.

OBSERVE

Keep predictions separate

Automatically saved identities remain unreviewed. Unknown images stay unknown until someone can identify them.

REVIEW

Retain human decisions

Confirm or correct a region in a photo. Save the original image and its reviewed interpretation as a training example.

TEACH

Promote explicitly

Adding an eligible reviewed image as an active reference is a separate, deliberate choice—not an automatic reward for confidence.

Reviews do not rewrite original predictions or historical egg totals. Private images, identities, and calibration stay out of the public repository.

The model does not get to mark its own homework. Neither do the hens.

09 / Useful on an ordinary Tuesday

Evidence you can inspect.
Automation you can trust cautiously.

In Home Assistant

Status shows the latest processed image and prediction separately from confirmed state. The journal keeps event evidence; MQTT exposes occupant, egg count, and latest event.

Under the hood

Bound capture time, protect credentials, persist nest state across restarts, and flag stale observations. Keep evidence retention and backups intentional.

No fresh observation ≠ an empty nest.
Stale live sensors become unavailable.

Visible egg counts need camera-specific calibration. An occupied-nest zero is not proof that there are no eggs underneath.

10 / The approach, in one sentence

♥♥♥

Observe carefully.
Infer conservatively.
Keep the receipts.

Use appearance to recognize hens, time to understand visits, and human review to build a better dataset.

We may not settle which came first.
We can make a better case for who came last.
